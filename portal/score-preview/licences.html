Score player source and licences

The score preview component uses a community WebAssembly build of MuseScore 4.6.5, pinned to @kreijstal/webmscore-wasm@4.6.5-build.1. Scores are processed in your browser. The score is not uploaded to an external conversion service.

The component and its inline API adapter are distributed under GPL-3.0-only. All modifications are provided as readable JavaScript beside this page. The engine, included fonts and SoundFont retain their own notices.

Original engraved pages stay unchanged. An inline translucent pink layer follows the current measure across all visible staves, including rests. Its geometry and timeline come from the original score Worker independently of optional note highlighting, and the player loads its versioned styles itself. Its layout uses native measure positions, staff lines and system boundaries; logical measures in a compressed multi-measure rest share its printed rectangle. The note layer stays separate so current note highlights remain visible. The base audio is the pinned engine’s written-score MP3. Playback follows MuseScore’s native repeat/ending itinerary by seeking that audio at repeat boundaries; its seek bar shows the written-score position and moves back at repeats. Note timing uses that same media clock, with MIDI tempo and per-staff/port performance events. Grace ownership and compressed multi-measure rests are preserved. Zero-duration grace notes retain beat-based seek targets. Two-note tremolos use their actual alternating MIDI gates. An isolated unsupported note or glyph does not disable tracking for the whole score. A fixed 51.179 ms compensation is measured for the pinned MP3 encoder; browser audio output latency can vary.

Upcoming pages are rendered and decoded ahead in a bounded six-page buffer, following the native repeat and ending itinerary. The first upcoming page is prepared before playback starts. Ready images swap at the boundary without waiting for another native render. Page turns retain the viewed instrument/staff and its screen offset where its engraved staff can be identified. When that staff is absent or hidden, the relative page position is preserved. The original eight-bar demo exercise is dedicated to the public domain under CC0. Fixture notice · Editable source.
